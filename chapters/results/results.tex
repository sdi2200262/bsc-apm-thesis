\section{Economic}
\label{sec:results-economic}

For nine of the twelve participants the APM session cost more in US-dollar-equivalent than the Spec-kit session, as Table~\ref{tab:cost-cohort} reports. The paired cost difference is the APM session cost minus the Spec-kit session cost, and all three summaries of its typical size are positive. The median and the Hodges-Lehmann estimate fall close together near six dollars, and the mean of the differences is somewhat higher, near seven. The Wilcoxon interval does not include zero, so the cohort-level direction holds. Its width, however, leaves the size of the difference imprecise.

The mean being higher than the median shows that a few large differences pull the typical size up, rather than the differences being spread evenly, as Figure~\ref{fig:cost-overall} shows. These large values concentrate in one model group, so the cost difference is read next within each model.

The cost difference behaved very differently between the two model groups, set out in Table~\ref{tab:cost-model} and shown in Figure~\ref{fig:cost-by-model}. Among the participants who worked with Opus, five of the six APM sessions cost more, and the difference was large on all three summaries. The median and the Hodges-Lehmann estimate are near twelve dollars and the mean is a little above. Among the participants who worked with Sonnet the difference was small on all three summaries and mixed in direction, pointing to the two frameworks costing about the same for that group.

The Opus group also varied widely, from one session where APM cost about four dollars less to one where it cost about thirty-two dollars more. At six participants the confidence interval is wide and includes zero, its bounds coinciding with those two extremes because at that sample size the interval reaches the smallest and largest differences rather than bounding the estimate more tightly. The Sonnet interval is similarly wide. Opus is the more expensive of the two models, so its differences fall on a larger dollar scale. Those large Opus values pulled the full-cohort summaries up, so the cohort-level cost difference comes mostly from the Opus group.

The two frameworks divided their spending differently across the task's two phases. As Table~\ref{tab:cost-phase} reports, APM's planning phase cost less than Spec-kit's and its implementation phase cost more.\footnote{A small residual the parser records as an ``other'' phase, session activity outside either framework's workflow phases, carries under one percent of cost and a difference indistinguishable from zero, and is omitted.} Both phase differences exclude zero. The implementation excess was the larger of the two and set the direction of the overall difference. The pattern held in both models, stronger for the Opus group and slighter for the Sonnet group. The per-model split is given in Table~\ref{tab:cost-phase-model} in Appendix~\ref{app:cost-breakdowns}.

Every role cost more under Opus than under Sonnet, matching the model's higher rate, while the order and rough proportions stayed the same.

The cost difference also divides by token category, the kind of token each dollar paid for, as Table~\ref{tab:cost-token} reports. As Section~\ref{sec:context-windows-and-context-decay} described, each call reprocesses the whole session so far, and caching lowers the price of the tokens that repeat. Under both frameworks Claude Code caches almost all of that prompt, so uncached input was negligible, a few cents either way, and the framework's effect falls on the cache and output categories rather than on fresh input.

The two cache-write rows separate the main conversations the user works in from the subagents those conversations dispatch. Claude Code writes the one-hour cache for a main conversation and the five-minute cache for a subagent. The one-hour writes therefore carry the framework comparison most directly, since the framework sets how many main agents the user runs.

Two categories carried the difference, output, and one-hour writes. APM produced more output than Spec-kit in all but one session and wrote more to the one-hour cache in every session, and for both categories the cohort confidence interval does not include zero. Both differences were larger under Opus than under Sonnet. The cache-read difference was mixed across the cohort, and its confidence interval includes zero. APM read more from the cache than Spec-kit under Opus, and less under Sonnet. The five-minute writes, the subagent cache, were a small positive difference. The per-model split of all five categories is given in Table~\ref{tab:cost-token-model} in Appendix~\ref{app:cost-breakdowns}.

Lastly, session order did not reverse the cost difference. APM cost more than Spec-kit whether participants met it first or second, in four of the six who used it first and five of the six who used it second. Table~\ref{tab:cost-order} in Appendix~\ref{app:cost-breakdowns} gives the difference within each order. The cost difference therefore tracks the framework rather than the order of the sessions.
